High Water Bill in Sacramento? Check for a Hidden Leak

A high water bill may reflect more usage, an estimated reading, an irrigation schedule or a hidden leak. Start by reviewing the billing period and checking what changed; avoid jumping directly to a costly slab repair without evidence.

Updated November 2026 · Practical homeowner guide

Three-step meter check

How to test for unexpected water use

  1. Stop normal water use. Turn off taps and avoid flushing toilets, laundry, dishwashers and irrigation. Check that automatic filling equipment is not running.
  2. Record the meter reading. Photograph the digits and low-flow indicator. Watching for 10–15 minutes can flag continuous movement, but cannot rule out a slow leak.
  3. Check again after two hours. If your household can safely avoid using water, the EPA advises comparing readings after a two-hour no-use period. A changed reading suggests unexpected flow and needs investigation.

Guidance: U.S. EPA WaterSense. An unexplained change may also come from an auto-fill, toilet or irrigation system rather than a slab leak.

Start with the water bill and meter

  • Compare the current reading period with previous bills
  • Check whether the bill used an estimate or an actual meter reading
  • Turn off taps, appliances and scheduled irrigation, then follow your utility’s leak-check guidance
  • Inspect toilets for silent running or a faulty flapper
  • If the meter still indicates flow, consider a professional plumbing assessment

Where hidden water can go

  • Behind a shower or tub wall after use
  • Through a leaking toilet or irrigation valve
  • Under a concrete slab or into soil around a buried line
  • Into crawlspaces or concealed ceiling cavities
  • Out through equipment drains or other non-obvious outlets
For account-specific alerts and meter guidance, visit the City of Sacramento water conservation website or your own water provider.

Questions people ask

Does the City of Sacramento offer leak alerts?+

The City publishes water conservation and water-use resources. Check the official utility website for current account features and eligibility.

Could my irrigation system be responsible?+

Yes. An automated controller or underground sprinkler leak may add significant usage without wet interior walls.

What if the meter moves with all taps off?+

Investigate toilet tanks, irrigation systems and any other connected uses. Persistent unexplained flow can warrant an inspection.
