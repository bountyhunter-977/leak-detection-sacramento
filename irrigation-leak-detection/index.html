Irrigation Leak Detection Sacramento

Irrigation leaks are easy to miss because many systems operate only a few times a week. A broken lateral, a leaking valve or a damaged drip line can waste water without creating an indoor symptom.

Why the source matters

Separating irrigation water use from household plumbing is an important first step. A wet patch may reflect a broken emitter, overspray, drainage problem or a line leak rather than a leak at the home’s main supply.

The right next step depends on what is leaking, where the line runs and whether another source can explain the symptoms.

Signs worth checking

  • Wet areas that appear only after a watering cycle
  • Unusually low pressure at one zone
  • Water seeping from a valve box after the cycle ends
  • A sprinkler zone that fails to shut off
  • A sudden increase in outdoor water consumption

How an investigation may proceed

Methods are selected based on site conditions and the provider's qualifications—not every job requires every test.

01

Observe each zone

Run zones one at a time and note pressure changes, runoff, geysers or damp spots.

02

Inspect valves and heads

Visible fittings, manifolds, emitters and valve boxes are checked before tracing buried piping.

03

Isolate the suspect circuit

A specialist may pressure test or trace the suspected zone to narrow the leak.

04

Repair and re-test

Damaged heads, fittings or laterals are addressed by an irrigation-qualified provider, followed by a zone check.

Questions people ask

Can sprinklers cause a high water bill?+

Yes. A valve that sticks or a buried lateral leak can increase usage, especially with automatic schedules.

Should I turn the controller off?+

If a leak is suspected, stopping watering can limit waste while the system is assessed. Some valves may need manual isolation.

Is irrigation leak detection the same as a plumbing leak?+

Not always. Sprinkler components and diagnostic methods differ, so confirm the provider can assess irrigation systems.
