How do you check for a hidden water leak?

Shut off all water use and watch your water meter. If a low-flow indicator continues to turn—or the smallest reading keeps changing—water may still be flowing. Check toilets, irrigation and appliances before concluding a pipe is leaking. See the complete local meter-check guide

Leak detection in Sacramento starts with the right clues

Not every wet patch is a pipe leak. The timing, location and water-meter pattern often help separate plumbing, irrigation, condensation and rain-related moisture.

  • The water meter moves when all fixtures are off
  • A room stays damp after the surface is dried
  • The floor feels warm in one unexpected area
  • Yard soil stays wet during dry weather
  • A pool loses more water than a basic bucket comparison suggests

One clue is worth a meter check. Two clues together — a warm floor and a rising bill — mean call before anyone digs.

How to check your water meter for a possible leak

Water-meter movement can reveal unexpected use, but it does not show whether a leak is under a slab, at a toilet, or outside. This is a screening step—not a professional diagnosis.

01

Switch off water use

Stop faucets, showers, laundry, dishwashers and scheduled irrigation. Check whether a toilet is refilling or any automatic system is operating.

02

Watch the indicator

When all water use is off, watch the meter’s low-flow or leak indicator for 10–15 minutes. Photograph the starting reading and note any movement.

03

Repeat for slow leaks

If nothing moves, a slow or intermittent leak is still possible. EPA recommends comparing meter readings over a two-hour period without using water. Follow your utility’s instructions.

Even a small leak wastes water quickly. A pinhole leak can waste roughly 5–20 gallons a day, and a moderate slab leak 50–100 gallons a day or more — which is why a rising bill is worth checking promptly.

Sacramento-specific context

Clay soil, older piping and the difference between clues and proof

Parts of the Sacramento region have clay-rich soils that can expand or contract with moisture. Soil movement, pipe age, installation details and corrosion conditions can all matter when investigating leaks—but none establishes that a particular home has a slab leak.

Sacramento water is also hard — about 8–14 grains per gallon — which can speed corrosion in older metal pipe. Pre-1970 homes, common in Midtown and Land Park, often still have their original supply lines.

LOCAL SOIL

Not every yard has the same soil

The USDA identifies a Sacramento clay soil series in certain basin areas. Clay behavior varies by parcel, drainage and construction; a property-specific evaluation matters more than a sweeping citywide claim.

USDA soil series documentation

PLUMBING MATERIAL

Do not guess the pipe’s age

Older homes can contain several generations of plumbing, including copper supply lines, repipes and repairs. A home built before the 1990s does not automatically have failing copper beneath its slab; confirm the material and history first.

What to request before anyone opens your floor

Some contractors can provide written findings and photos; others may only provide a verbal diagnosis. Ask about the deliverable before you book. We cannot promise a report or specific diagnostic equipment on behalf of an unnamed provider.

Testing scope. Which supply lines, fixtures or pool circuits will be isolated and tested?

Evidence. Will you receive readings, photos, marked locations and any uncertainties in writing?

Repair choices. Is a local spot repair feasible, or should rerouting or repiping be evaluated?

Documentation. Can the provider supply findings relevant to an insurance claim if requested?

Visit length. Most detection visits take 1–2 hours; ask what the visit covers and whether findings are explained on site.

Permits. Detection itself needs no permit; repair that opens a slab or replaces a main water line may need one. Ask who arranges it.

Before you arrange an inspection

Can a leak be located without opening a wall or floor?+

Many first-line tests do not require opening surfaces, but some leaks need access or further confirmation. A responsible inspection should explain uncertainty.

Is slab leak detection different from pool leak detection?+

Yes. Pool shells, skimmers and pool plumbing can require specialty tests not used on household supply lines.

Does detecting a leak mean it will be repaired right away?+

Not automatically. Detection and repair may be separate scopes, and not every specialist provides both.

What if I have an active flood?+

Shut off the water if safe, avoid electrical hazards and contact appropriate emergency help. This website is not an emergency dispatch line.
