Underground Water Leak Detection Sacramento

A leaking underground service line may waste water for weeks before you see a puddle. Unusual meter activity, persistently damp soil, a drop in pressure or an unexplained high bill can all justify a closer look at the buried supply between a meter and the home.

How do you check for an underground water-line leak?

If the water meter shows continuous usage while all fixtures and irrigation are off, an unseen leak on the property is one possibility. Look for persistently wet ground, lower water pressure or higher-than-normal water bills. A technician may isolate the service line and use listening or line-tracing equipment; the meter test alone does not show where a leak is located.

Source and reference: Sacramento County Water Agency meter guidance
Confirm water use

Turn off fixtures, irrigation and water-using appliances, then watch the meter.

Check responsibility

Ask the local water provider where public utility responsibility ends and private service-line responsibility begins.

Locate before excavation

A service-line investigation can reduce unnecessary digging, but cannot guarantee zero excavation.

Why the source matters

Before digging, identify the water supplier and which portion of the line is on the customer side of the meter. Utility ownership and repair responsibility can vary. Call 811 before any excavation and have the utility confirm its requirements.

The right next step depends on what is leaking, where the line runs and whether another source can explain the symptoms.

Signs worth checking

  • A water meter that continues moving with fixtures off
  • Soft or unusually green patches in an otherwise dry yard
  • Falling water pressure without an indoor leak
  • Pooling water near the buried service-line route
  • Water-bill increases that cannot be explained by irrigation

How an investigation may proceed

Methods are selected based on site conditions and the provider's qualifications—not every job requires every test.

01

Check meter activity

First rule out toilets, irrigation and other fixtures that can make a meter move.

02

Identify the pipe route

Service-line drawings, visible entry points and locating equipment can help establish a plausible route.

03

Isolate and test

Pressure testing or acoustic methods may help distinguish an underground supply leak from other water use.

04

Plan safe access

A repair contractor can assess excavation or line replacement after utilities are marked and responsibility is confirmed.

Questions people ask

Is the water utility responsible for a leak in my yard?+

Not necessarily. The ownership boundary depends on the utility and meter arrangement. Contact your water provider before assuming responsibility.

Can an underground leak be found without digging?+

Some leaks can be narrowed through acoustic, pressure or tracer techniques, but an excavation may still be required to confirm and repair them.

Do I need to call 811?+

Before excavation, contact 811 so participating utility-owned lines can be marked. Private lines may need additional locating.
