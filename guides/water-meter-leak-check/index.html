How to Check Your Water Meter for a Hidden Leak

If your water bill is higher than usual but no pipe is visibly leaking, a meter check can help show whether water is still moving through the system when everything is supposed to be off.

Updated November 13, 2026 · Based on Sacramento County Water Agency guidance

Answer in 30 seconds

Can a water meter tell you if you have a leak?

Often, yes—but it cannot tell you exactly where the leak is. If the low-flow indicator rotates with all water use off, or the lowest meter digit keeps changing, water may be passing through the meter. A running toilet, irrigation valve, appliance or other unexpected use can create the same reading. Rule these out before assuming a buried or slab pipe is leaking.

Reference: Sacramento County Water Agency — Water Metering / Check for Leaks.

Three steps to check the meter

  1. Stop intentional water use. Turn off faucets, showers, dishwashers, washing machines, irrigation schedules and any other water-using equipment. Ask everyone in the home not to use water briefly. Do not shut off essential or safety equipment.
  2. Find the indicator or lowest digit. Some analog meters have a small rotating low-flow symbol; some digital meters show changing digits. Meter designs vary. If a cover is unsafe or inaccessible, contact the utility instead of forcing it.
  3. Watch for 10–15 minutes and record what changes. Take a before-and-after photo. Continued movement means unexpected flow is possible, not that a slab leak has been proven. Repeat after checking toilets and irrigation if appropriate.

What does the result mean?

Meter does not move

No continuous usage was observed during your check. Intermittent leaks can still be present.

Meter moves with everything off

Check toilets, irrigation valves, softeners and connected appliances. If movement continues, professional investigation may be justified.

Meter or supply line seems damaged

Contact your water provider for its safety and responsibility instructions rather than opening or altering utility equipment.

When should a Sacramento homeowner call a leak specialist?

If the meter continues showing flow after common fixtures and irrigation are ruled out, or if there is new foundation moisture, an unexplained warm floor or pressure loss, ask a qualified contractor about pressure isolation and non-destructive diagnostic options. Not every leak requires acoustic or tracer-gas testing.

For a suspected slab leak, compare testing and repair options. For wet ground near a buried line, read the underground water-line guide. For a pool, start with the pool leak guide instead; pool-system testing is different.
